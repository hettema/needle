## Dispositions

1. **A resume forks the session id.** The floor kept the id on `--resume`, so the lane, the rescue ledger and the slot record were keyed to an id that dies at the first Answer; live, a94f09d3 resumed as d113504c on the same slot. FIXED (rulings 3): the lane follows its worktree's chain of ids, the ledger is written under the id that lives, the fake forks, and five tests that asserted a kept id were rewritten to assert the fork.
2. **The registry's word is stale across a resume, and stamped after the hook fires.** A resumed session read `blocked` with the previous turn's detail after replying THANKS; the hook's Stop carried THANKS and was dated a moment before the registry's stamp. FIXED (ruling 4): a Stop the hook pushed within a minute of the registry's stamp wins over a registry that is not `working`.
3. **A stopped lane read as folded, trunk synced and main synced.** Its zero-commit branch, deleted with the worktree, was an ancestor of origin/develop from birth and the reflog that would have said so was gone. FIXED (ruling 7): the lane record carries its birth commit from the first sighting, and a fold is the tip having moved from it; a branch with no birth known is unprovable, never folded.
4. **A card whose worktree the owner removed still said "already exists".** The door read the lane record's path. FIXED (ruling 14): the lane's path is the disk's; a gone worktree offers Start and closes Look and Resume with the reason.
5. **A card said "Moved to fable on hrclaude" for an Answer two lives back.** The rescue ledger holds every move, including a resume that stayed on its rung. FIXED: "Moved" is said only for a change of rung after the lane's current life began.
6. **A stopped lane's reason read "the journal says: Consumed 2.6 s CPU time".** The scope's accounting line matched the telling words. FIXED (ruling 16): the journal speaks only for a kill or a failure line.
7. **Two windows for one rescue.** A test called the lane loop directly while the registry watcher ran it under the lock; both rescued. FIXED (ruling 5): every door and every read of the machine take the loops' one lock, and the test goes through it.
8. **A card read Done before its close was asserted.** The loops' timers ran their work at once, so the signal loop's first tick landed between a test's close and its assertion. FIXED (ruling 17): the loops run once, in order, before the board is served; the timers wait first.
9. **A rescue's new window was refused as "already has a window".** The stop that ended the attach window had not been recorded as a close. FIXED: a rescue records the old window closed before opening the new one (recording a close the stop caused is not closing one).

## What the build learned the plan got wrong

- The plan's item 4 named an "Open the plan / the record" door that opens the document in the owner's reader. Slice 01's ruling 16 keeps the render in-card because a browser blocks `file://` from an http page and an `xdg-open` through the runtime has no effect the board can prove; that ruling stands (ruling 15) and the door is the in-card render already there.
- The plan's item 7 asked for a rescue ledger "separate from the record of where the lane lives". The runtime's ledger and slot record already were (slice 02); this slice adds the board's own lane record (`lanes`) beside them, and it is that record — not the runtime's — that carries a lane's birth, fold and sync facts.
- The plan's item 3 says the card shows the lane's state "from those events and the runtime's session list together". Together turned out to mean three sources with an order: the process (live or not), the registry's word, and the hook's Stop when it is the newest; the registry alone already carries a stopped question as `blocked` with the question as its detail.
