2. **Codex's pass — the reader who was not the author.** Called warm through `needle call` (#59) on commit d72cab7, the frozen revision; it opened all twelve changed files and ran an isolated probe of the sweep. 3. **The fixed work again.** Findings 1 to 3 landed as one rework of `_sweep_scopes`: a group is asked to be ended only after it has read nobody-home with both guards holding on every read for thirty seconds, timed by the clock and not counted in reads, and the clock starts again the moment a guard fails; the ask is `systemctl stop --no-block`, so the beat never waits on the manager; the card is told only on the read that finds the group empty, a refusal is said once in words that claim no stop and asked again after another window, and a group still not empty a window after the ask is asked again. Re-reading the reworked loop found one more (finding 5): a read where the manager could not be asked answered `None`, which the emptiness check would have read as empty and written a *Stopped* row for; it now waits for a read that can be made. On Codex's list of the unexercised: ancestry has a unit test at the board level (`tests/board/test_dial.py`), with a stale copy owning nothing and an empty group being nobody's leftovers; refusal and recovery have their own test; restart is the in-memory memo starting again, which is the conservative side and is stated in the loop's docstring; launch timing is what the window is for; and that the floor's manager ends no process is stated on the floor itself — the three by-hand stops on this laptop, one of which took the manager's whole timeout, are what shows a real stop ends what a group holds. The test for item 1 now also puts a session's turn to `done` with its process standing and reads a whole window with nothing stopped.

## What the build learned the plan got wrong

- The plan asked for a stop on the next reconcile. One read is a doubt: a group read empty of sessions can be a session settling into it, so the stop waits for a second read thirty seconds on. The plan's item 1 and its rulings were amended in the lane before the build.
- The plan said a group is home when a live session's pid is in it. A session's children can sit in the group while its own pid sits elsewhere, so ownership follows ancestry; and the leftover the live read found was the other way round — a server whose session was gone — which ancestry reads correctly as nobody's.
