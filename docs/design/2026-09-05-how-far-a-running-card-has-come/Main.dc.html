The calls this comp makes
The session's — made, open to veto
1. The count is the lane's word. The card shows what the lane marked in its plan and never what the board inferred from commits or the transcript; the word is "met", the stance the close-out already uses, not "done".
2. The strip lives between the essence and the state line, on Executing cards only. The state line, the door and the border are untouched, so every signed state reads as it did.
3. Met segments take the live colour, the lane's own. A deviated item is outlined, never filled. Nothing is green: green is the board's word for a loop it closed itself.
4. A plan with no items shows nothing new. Zero met is shown: "0 of 4 met" after an hour is the signal that a lane is not marking, and hiding it would hide the gap.
5. Once every item is met and a review record exists in the lane, the line under the strip becomes the review counter — pass, found, fixed, filed — read from the record's own head and lists. It paints the broken colour while no pass has come clean, because that is what red means here: something found wrong and the loop not yet closed. It goes quiet on the first clean pass. It is never green: a clean pass is the lane's word, and green is the board's.
Yours — asked, with the trade
1. The last met item's title on the face, or the count alone? The title costs one line on every Executing card and buys knowing where the lane is without opening it. Drawn with the title; the strip and count alone is the alternative.
2. Does the strip stay on the face after Executing — Decision moment, Executed, Done — as the record of how the run went? Drawn for Executing only, with the open card carrying the items in every column. The alternative is a strip that never changes again on every shipped card.
3. The mark a session writes at the item: "Met:" with what shows it, "Deviated:" with a pointer. This is the close-out stance written early, one grammar for both. The alternative is a checkbox, which no plan in either corpus uses and which carries no evidence.
4. The review counter in red while the loop is open, or quiet throughout? Red says at a glance: built, and still finding things. The trade is red under every card in review for an hour or two, against the phase being invisible until you read the line. A sixth colour for "in review" is not offered: it would be a colour for a category, which the five-word rule you signed refuses.
