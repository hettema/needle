What I want you to judge

Nine calls. Six are mine and I have made them — they are here so you can veto, not so you can choose. Three are yours: they change what the board is, not how it looks. Hover a number on the page (turn Notes on) to see where each lives.

  1. The first inch answers "does anything need me?" Four counts, and only four colours ever appear: amber is your move, red is the board is not true right now, teal is structure, green is landed. Everything else is grey. 0.1 painted every alarm red and the red became wallpaper. One red means one thing: a fact on this page is wrong. In this slice only two things can be wrong — a card citing a document that is nowhere, and a write that failed. Arrivals are counted here too. Your ruling of tonight makes the corpus the only way in: a plan any session writes anywhere becomes a card the second the file lands, with no click. So cards appear while you are looking at the board, and a card that appeared silently is a card you never read. It carries a NEW mark for its first day, and the count says how many. The header now says watching rather than when it last read, because an idle watching board and a frozen one look identical otherwise — that one cost 0.1 a day. Mine. NEW ages out after a day, which needs nothing remembered about you. If you would rather it read new since you last looked, that is one stored timestamp and I will build it instead — say which.
  2. The column definition is one hover away, and it names who moves the card. Hovering a column head opens its ruling — what the column means, plus a line saying whose move it is: yours, or the machine's from slice 02. The grammar is the thing a new session and a returning owner both get wrong. Putting "moved by you / moved by the machine" in the definition is what makes lesson 2 — true without anyone remembering — readable rather than remembered. Mine. The wording of all eight definitions is in the comp; read them and correct any that are not your ruling.
  3. A card at rest is number, title, one line of essence, gate. Not chips-only as in 0.1. The essence is the plan's own first sentence of intent, clamped to two lines. 0.1 hid SERVES behind an expand, so scanning a column meant reading titles alone and titles are not enough to rank by. This is the single biggest change to how the board reads. Yours: is two lines of essence right, or do you want one, or three? It sets how many cards fit a screen — about 6 per column at two lines, 8 at one.
  4. Every card says what is written behind it — five states, always one. Plan · Suggestion · Archived · Note — nothing written yet · Document gone (red). Your intent says a card with no plan or written suggestion behind it is a note and the board must say so. That makes "note" a first-class kind rather than a defect — your own decision queue lives there legitimately. Document gone is the only one that is an error, and it is the red. Yours: is Note the right word for a card carrying no document? Alternatives: Unwritten, Ask, or nothing at all.
  5. The drop preview is a real gap, and it says where the card lands. The card lifts under the cursor; a gap opens at the insertion point with the accent line above it and the words "#196 lands here — rank 2". Ranks below renumber live. "A dropped card lands where the preview said" is in the plan as a done-means. A gap that shows the rank makes that checkable by eye instead of by trust. Mine. A drop into a grouped column lands inside the group it falls in — never a phantom group at the top. That was 0.1's review finding 8 and it carries over as a test.
  6. Keyboard reordering is a first-class door, not an accessibility footnote. Focus a card, space to lift, arrows to move within and across columns, space to drop, escape to cancel. The hint appears on the focused card only. Mine. It is also the only way to reorder precisely on a trackpad, which is why it is on the card rather than in a help page.
  7. A write that fails is shown as failed, on the card, with the reason. The card stays exactly where it was — it never appears in the new place and then snaps back — and carries the store's own words plus a Retry. The page never shows a state the store does not hold. 0.1's deepest trap was a toast saying "saved" over a write that had eaten the owner's typed idea. There is no optimistic rendering anywhere in Needle; the store is local and a move is a few milliseconds, so honesty costs nothing visible. Mine, and it is a ratchet, not a convention.
  8. Sorting is a lens; only dragging writes. Rank · Age · Gate at the top right. Choosing Age changes what you see and never the stored order; the column head keeps saying rank so you always know whether what you are looking at is the priority. Mine. A sort that wrote would destroy the ranking you built, silently, the first time you looked at the board by age.
  9. Executed, Done and Not now start furled on a laptop. They open to full columns on a click and stay open. On a wide screen all eight start open. Eight columns is 2,400 pixels. On your laptop the five working columns fit together only if the archived cards give up their width. The rails keep the count visible so nothing is hidden, only narrow. Yours: this is the one place I trade "everything on the plate, at once" against fitting the screen. If you want all eight always open and to scroll instead, say so — it is a one-line change and I will not argue.

What is not here, on purpose. No Start, no Discuss, no Answer, no Watch; no lanes strip, no bands, no run-out banner, no toasts. Those are the runtime, and they arrive in slices 02 and 03. Judging the surface with none of them present is the point of doing this slice first: if the board is not already better than 0.1 for reading and planning with nothing running behind it, the sequence stops here and the plan is amended.

The design system. Every colour, size and radius on this page is a token in this file's own stylesheet, and the build copies that token set into frontend/src/components/ui/ once. From then on the folder is the single living source and a raw colour outside it is refused by a test — this page stays exactly as you signed it, a record of the sitting rather than a second stylesheet quietly restyling itself behind you.
