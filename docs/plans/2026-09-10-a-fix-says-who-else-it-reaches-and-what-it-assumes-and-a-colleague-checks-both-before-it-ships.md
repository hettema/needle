## Intent

A review ends when the work holds, and the fixes a review makes are held to the same bar as the build. Today a fix is scoped by the finding a reader showed and stands on whatever its writer assumed about the rest of the system, and the only thing that catches a fix that missed its sibling, broke another reader of the same surface, or stood on a wrong premise is the next full pass, an hour later. On Hello Revenue #456 that turned a two-hour build into thirty-six hours of review: thirteen of thirty-four passes opened on the previous round's own repair, and twelve of those thirteen were findable at fix time by a search over the tree or by driving the real path. The suite was green after every one, because a fix's author writes its tests from the fix's own premise.

After this card, every fix line in a review record says who else the fix reaches and what it assumes; a colleague of the other kind tries to break exactly those two claims before the round ships, and the record quotes the verdict; a finding caused by an earlier fix says so on its line, so the board can count how often a review chases its own repairs; and a record that skipped any of this cannot close a card, in any project on the board. The rule itself is already written — §13 says a repair names its dependencies and a cold reader asks who else calls this — and is held by nothing; this card makes it held. What the owner gets: fewer review rounds, because the fix that would have cost the next pass is caught in the minutes after it is written, and a number on the fifth Hello Revenue close after this ships that says whether that happened.

What does not change: no cap on passes — the loop still ends by §13's rule of 2026-09-09, at whatever count the work earns; records written before this ships are history and are not rewritten; Hello Revenue's `hr-feature-review` skill points at §13 and restates nothing, and stays as it is.

## Rulings

1. **The colleague's verdict is a gate at the close, not a note the next pass may read.** The owner left this call to the plan. Rejected: the suggestion's first form, a quoted verdict with the five-close loop deciding later whether it should block — §5 says a boundary whose failure is silent or late gets a mechanism, and this one fails both ways: a round shipped unread looks exactly like a round read, and the register's §13 line has claimed since 2026-09-05 that the repair rule is read by the next pass, which #456 shows it was not. So the close refuses, for a record dated on or after this card's fold: a FIXED line with no reach or no premise; a round of repairs with no cold reader's verdict quoted; and a last round whose verdict is not complete. The cost is nothing new — every round's repairs already owe a cold read before they ship (§13, 2026-09-09); what is new is that a record that skipped it cannot close a card.
2. **The refusal sits at the close, where the record is already read, and so holds every project's records.** `api/doors.py::close` already refuses a code lane without its record; the same door reads the record's lines. Rejected: a refusal at the fold — the fold knows no record path, a fold without a close already lands the card on the owner's attention rather than on shipped (§14), and a second place that judges the record is a second way. Rejected: a ratchet alone — it would hold Needle's records and nobody else's, and the pattern that prompted this card is Hello Revenue's. Needle's own records are held at test time too, by a ratchet that calls the same reader the door does, so there is one grammar and one reader.
3. **The record's date decides, as the class rule's does.** A record whose filename date is before the fold is exempt and does not count in the loop, whatever passes it runs afterwards: the lanes in flight at the fold wrote their early rounds under the old form and refusing them at close would be a rule applied backwards. Hello Revenue's five judged closes are therefore lanes whose record began after the fold — the clean measure, at the price of a slower signal.
4. **The grammar has one source and one reader.** The form of the fix line, the verdict line and the repair-caused mark lives in Needle's `docs/reviews/README.md`, the file that already holds the class rule; Hello Revenue's `docs/reviews/README.md` carries a copy that cites it, as it does for the class rule today. The one reader is `board/parse.py::review_of`, which already reads passes and dispositions onto the card; the door, the card's face, the ratchet and the loop's reading all read through it. Rejected: a second regex in the door or the ratchet — the class ratchet has its own today and that is a debt this card does not add to.
5. **The reader falsifies the lines, not the diff.** The completeness read of a round is handed the round's FIXED lines and has one job per line: re-run the search behind the reach and name a sibling or a reader the line left out; drive the premise and say what it saw. Rejected: a fourth full pass per round — a full pass is what costs the hour, and its first finding on #456 was the repair thirteen times because nobody asked the narrow question. The reader is of the other kind and is reached through `needle call` and `wait` (§12); the four questions it answers — who else calls this, what is the sibling case, what list was written by hand, what does the fix assume and what drove it — are named in the one text, and the sentence that hands a lane the job lives once, in the text every lane opens with.
6. **The doctrine words are the owner's to rule at Start; the plan proposes them.** The suggestion's `Fix:` line said so. Proposed for §13, replacing the clause "and the affected dependencies; it changes enough to remove the cause and reconcile those dependencies": *"and the affected dependencies — who else the fix reaches and what it assumes about who else reads or writes the thing, written on the fix's own line; it changes enough to remove the cause and reconcile those dependencies"*. Proposed for the cold reader's clause, replacing "who else calls this, what is the sibling case, what list was written by hand": *"who reads the fix's line and tries to break it — who else calls this, what is the sibling case, what list was written by hand, what does the fix assume and what drove it — and whose verdict is on the record before the round ships"*. Two sentences change and none is added. Ruled at this door, 2026-09-10: "Wording change looks fine to me. Let's try it." — the words above stand as written, and the lane commits the edit naming #110 (the doctrine hook, `hooks/commit-msg`). The same words set the loop's cadence to daily: "lets let the loop check every day. We develop a lot, I wouldn't be surprised if we cross the 5 today already."

## Items

### 1. Every fix line says who else it reaches and what it assumes, and a record that skips it cannot close a card
A FIXED line in a review record dated on or after this card's fold says, beside what was done and in free words, who else the fix reaches — the sibling cases it covers, the other readers of the surface it changed — and what it assumes about who else writes or reads the thing and in what order. The form is set once in Needle's `docs/reviews/README.md` beside the class rule (proposed: `— FIXED in <sha>; reaches <…>; assumes <…>`; the lane settles the words and records why), copied with its citation into Hello Revenue's README, and read by `board/parse.py::review_of` into the `Review` type, whose frontend mirror follows. The close refuses a dated record with a FIXED line that lacks either half, naming the record and the line, in the voice it uses for a missing record; a ratchet under `tests/ratchets/` holds Needle's own records through the same reader. Done means: on the door fixture, a code lane whose record dated after the fold has one FIXED line with no reach refuses to close naming that line, and the same record with both halves closes; a record dated before the fold with bare FIXED lines closes as today; Needle's ratchet is red on a fixture record with a bare line and green on the corpus; `npx tsc --noEmit` fails if the `Review` type grows on one side only; the three real corpora read through the reader without error. Hands out: `execution` — the reader run over Needle's, Hello Revenue's and the machine's review folders, tabling per record the FIXED lines and how many carry both halves; verifies by opening two records the table says carry none and one it says carries some.

### 2. A colleague of the other kind tries to break each fix line before the round ships, and the record quotes the verdict
The text every lane session opens with — the Start door's brief in `api/doors.py`, beside the filing rule it already carries in one sentence — says, once, what the completeness read of a round is: the round's FIXED lines handed to a colleague of the other kind through `needle call`, one job per line — re-run the search behind the reach and name a sibling or a reader the line left out; drive the premise and say what it saw — and a verdict per round that names each line it broke, quoted in the record under `## The passes` in the form the README sets (proposed: one line per round, `Read cold by <who, on what> on <sha>: complete` or `: broke <finding numbers> — <its words>`; the lane settles it). The close refuses a dated record with a round of repairs and no verdict, and one whose last round's verdict is not complete. Done means: the brief text names the four questions and the verb, and a resumed lane's brief carries the same sentence; on the door fixture a dated record with a FIXED round and no verdict line refuses to close naming the round, one whose last verdict broke a line refuses naming the line, and one whose last verdict is complete closes; a throwaway round — one fix line whose stated premise is false on a scratch project — is handed to a real Codex reader through `needle call`, and its answer names the premise it drove and found false, kept verbatim in this card's review record. Hands out: `execution` — the throwaway call, its answer file, and the reading session's transcript lines, verbatim; verifies by reading the answer file before the item claims the reader drove anything.

### 3. A finding caused by an earlier fix says so on its line, and the board counts them

## Loop

**The baseline, read 2026-09-10 by the closing session of Hello Revenue #456, from the record's own words.** Hello Revenue's `docs/reviews/2026-09-08-landing-pages-look-like-the-client-and-improve-with-every-test.md`: 34 passes, 251 FIXED lines; thirteen passes' first finding was the previous round's own repair (passes 2, 5, 7, 9, 10, 11, 18, 20, 21, 23, 31, 32, 33 — each says so in its opening sentence); of the 251 fix lines, 13 name any reach and 1 names a premise. Review records since 2026-09-05, by `board.parse.review_of`: 157, 34, 27, 20, 19, 18, 17 and 16 passes on eight records; before 2026-09-05 the norm was 3 to 8. The repair-caused count is read by hand from the passes' opening sentences because no line carried a mark; it is a lower bound, and item 3 is what makes the next count a machine's.

We think a fix that states its reach and its premise, checked by a colleague of the other kind who tries to break both before the round ships, will change the number of findings a review makes on its own previous repairs — from thirteen of thirty-four passes on #456 to at most one per review — because twelve of those thirteen were findable at fix time by a search or a driven path, and a fix's own tests cannot catch a premise their author holds. Passes per review then fall to what the work earns, and the review's hours with them.

Fixed now, before the data: the sample is the first five Hello Revenue review records dated on or after this card's fold, read at their closes through `review_of` from Needle's checkout by a session the board starts — per record its passes, its repair-caused findings and the lines its cold reader broke per round. The observation that discriminates: repair-caused findings fall while the reader keeps breaking lines — the reader is doing what the next pass used to do, earlier. If repair-caused findings stay above one per review on average and the reader breaks little, the writer is filling the lines without the act and the representation is wrong: the door then reads the lines against the diff (every file the diff touches that the reach line does not name), and this card reopens. If the reader breaks nothing while repair-caused findings continue, the reader is reading and not driving: its brief changes to require the driven path, and this card reopens. If repair-caused findings are at or under one per review, the rule stays and this loop closes. The count of passes and the review's wall-clock hours from the commits' author dates are read beside it, because the owner's pain was the hours; they are context, not the judge, since the work in five closes will not be the same work.

## Deliberately not

- A cap on passes. The loop ends by §13's stop rule of 2026-09-09; this card changes what a round costs, not how many the work may earn.
- Rewriting records dated before the fold, in any project. The date decides (ruling 3).
- A refusal at the fold, or the door reading the fix lines against the diff. The first is a second judge of the record (ruling 2); the second is the loop's action on failure and is written above, not built now.
- Editing Hello Revenue's `hr-feature-review` skill, or its `CLAUDE.md`. The skill points at §13 and restates nothing.
- A new verb that counts reviews across records. The reader exists (`review_of`) and the loop's session runs it by script; #16 names the two verbs the parked loops need, and this loop is not one of them.
- Moving the class ratchet onto the shared reader. Its own regex is a debt this card names and does not pay; it is outside the change.
- Any change to how a colleague is called. `needle call` and `wait` are the channel (§12, card #51); the reader's job is a sentence in a brief, not a mechanism.
