## Intent

A session on any project, of any make, works under the doctrine as reliably as a Hello Revenue session did under its own file, and the owner can trust that without checking. The one text becomes the base every project reads, built on Hello Revenue's `CLAUDE.md` as its model: its shape, its doctrine paragraphs, and the carriers that held them there. The text gets shorter, because the shape does the arguing. The rules that failed since the switch, verification against ground truth first, are in the text with the discriminator that makes them usable, are re-anchored in every session where they used to be re-anchored in one project, and are named in the register with what holds them. And the whole change is a thesis with its measurement fixed before the text lands, so in ten closes we know whether the text was the lever.

Hello Revenue's `CLAUDE.md` was a source for neither. The suggestion this plan carries said so and filed itself as the successor.
- **The verification rule was only ever Hello Revenue's.** "Ground truth" appears four times in Hello Revenue's file, never in the former global file (omarchy-machine `1cabeb0:home/.claude/CLAUDE.md`, 2,300 words), never in the one text (3,500 words). The rule that a subagent's summary, training recall and in-session memory are not ground truth, that a hedge is a trigger to verify, that the naming impulse is the moment to search, and that the search obligation binds the plan before the execution, has never been doctrine outside Hello Revenue. Every Needle and every Codex session has worked without it.
- **Hello Revenue holds that rule with more than text.** `.claude/hooks/backbrief.sh` re-injects the drift-prone rules on the word `backbrief`, verify first (its header says why: the file's rules fade in salience as a session grows); `.claude/output-styles/general-brief.md` shapes every answer; `hr-plan-write`, `hr-plan-execute` and `hr-feature-review` carry the intents into the acts; `scripts/method_register.py` gives every method a stance and `test_method_register_is_total.py` keeps it in agreement with the file. All of it is project-local: a Needle or Codex session never receives the re-anchor. Needle's own hook (`hooks/needle_hook.py`) is global on this machine, for the board's events only; its ratchet (`tests/ratchets/test_one_rule_one_hook.py`) holds that its stdout is the board's word and nothing else.
- **Hello Revenue's head points at a base that is gone.** Its first paragraph says the doctrine lives in the global file and is not repeated here, and names the former global file's sections. That base was replaced by the one text, whose sections differ. Hello Revenue sessions read a local file layered on a doctrine it was not written against.
- **The shape is the difference, not the length.** Hello Revenue's doctrine paragraphs each carry an intent, the ground truth that makes it hard, the current holder, and the incident that shaped it, dated. The one text argues its rules in prose. The shape is shorter per rule and harder to misread, and a rule with a dated failure is one a reader can weigh against a new one.
- **The doctrine's own rules bind this plan.** A doctrine edit lands on a card and the commit names it (`hooks/commit-msg`, `tests/ratchets/test_a_doctrine_edit_lands_on_a_card.py`); a plan that edits the constitution in its own commit is refused, so this plan lands first and the lane's commits name #60. `docs/HOW-WE-HOLD-IT.md` and the one text's section titles must agree both ways (`tests/ratchets/test_every_rule_says_what_holds_it.py`), so a rewrite that moves a section moves the register in the same commit. A method becomes doctrine only when the person and a colleague have aligned on it (§2), so the owner reads the full text, not a summary of it, before it lands.

## Loop

We think rebuilding the one text on Hello Revenue's shape with its verification and search rules, and delivering the re-anchor to every session of every make, will move the verification-class findings per carded close on Needle from today's rate to at or below Hello Revenue's, because those rules were absent from the text outside Hello Revenue and their re-anchor was project-local, not because sessions are careless. Fixed before the data: the baseline is item 5's count, written here by the lane before item 2 lands, per project; the sample is the next ten carded closes across Needle and Hello Revenue after the fold, read from their review records and the cross-make notes. The observation that discriminates: the drop appears on Needle and in Codex sessions, which never had the rule, and not on Hello Revenue, which already had it. If Hello Revenue's rate falls as much as Needle's, the lever was something else (the brevity, or the owner's attention while this was being built) and the finding about the missing rule does not travel. If after ten closes Needle's rate is not at or below Hello Revenue's baseline, the text is not the lever: the holder for verification becomes a check that refuses (a backbrief that names no source is refused at the door), the register's line changes from traced to held, and this card reopens. The owner is asked once, at the tenth close, with the counts attached, whether the working relation reads as restored; his answer is the judgment of taste the counts cannot give.
