## Intent

When you turn auto-fix on, a verified defect gets a plan and a session without you, and the card moves with the work. Today the first card the switch took on Hello Revenue got its plan and then became yours with its Start closed: the plan was pushed from the rented machine, the board waited two minutes for it while it levels its own copy every five, called the session ended "without a plan", read the plan in minutes later, and by its own rule left the card yours from there; and the plan's title, written under the same words a cold reading had already refused on the suggestion, failed the reading again, so Start is closed on a card nobody will rewrite. What you lose: a slot of the four spent on a card that ends on your desk unstartable, with the reading's advice addressed to a writer who has gone, and a "fixing themselves" count that stalls while it looks busy.

After this plan, a planning session is judged on what reached the board, not on a clock: when its turn ends, the board levels its own copy of the project at once, whatever machine the session ran on, and reads the plan in; only a levelling that ran after the turn and found nothing ends the session. A plan that arrives after the board had already given up re-opens the card's work instead of leaving it yours. A planning session for a card whose title you could not place is told, at the top of its brief, which words failed and your test, so the plan's title is in your words the first time; and if a reading still refuses the new title, the board hands that reading back to the same session to rewrite, as many times as its hour allows. Only when the hour runs out with the title still failing is the card yours — with the reading's words on its face, and Start opening by itself the moment a reading of your title passes.

What does not change: the title test and who reads titles, the readings cap, the five-minute loop that levels every machine's copy, the number of fix lanes, and the rule that a card the dial took once is not taken again.

## Items

### 1. A planning session's turn is settled against what reached the board, not a clock
When a planning session's turn ends with no plan and no question on the card, the beat levels the board's own copy of the project at once — the one-project level the fold and the terminal use, one fetch per turn's end and never one per beat — whatever machine the session ran on, and the corpus is read in. The session is called ended without a plan only when a levelling that ran after the turn ended found nothing: the trunk record's read time against the turn's end, never a number of seconds. A level the checkout refused — uncommitted work, not on develop, origin unreachable — is not "nothing arrived": the beat writes the refusal on the card and waits, within the planning hour, and the hour's end is what stops it. The card's history names the level that settled it. Done means: on the test board with the floor's second machine, a planning session placed there whose plan is pushed to origin from a clone of the fixture's repository, levelled by nothing else, is not ended — the beat's own level reads it in, the lane reaches Planned and the card starts; a session whose turn ends with nothing pushed is ended only after a level that ran after its turn found nothing, and the card's history says so; a level the checkout refuses leaves the lane planning with the refusal on the card, and the hour ends it; `test_a_planning_session_that_dies_ends_the_dials_part_and_the_card_says_why` holds unchanged; `PLANNING_SETTLE_SECONDS` has no reader left.

### 2. A plan that arrives after the board gave up on its session re-opens the card's work
A fix lane the beat ended without a plan, whose card now carries a plan that carries its suggestion, goes back to Planned on the next beat, with a line on the card's history saying the plan arrived after the session was called ended, and Start is opened as for any plan the dial wrote. The card is still one the dial took once — it is not taken again — but it is not yours for the wrong reason. This reaches the cards already on your desk from this failure: Hello Revenue #453 re-opens on the first beat after the fold and the restart. Done means: on the test board, a lane ended without a plan whose plan lands afterwards reads Planned on the next beat, its history names the arrival, it starts when its Start is offered, and it is not planned a second time; `needle fixes` shows no ended lane on a card that carries a plan; on the served board after the restart, #453's lane reads Planned, its Start held by its title, which is yours to rewrite because its writer is gone (ruling 6), and Start opens by itself once a reading of your title passes.

### 3. A planning session is told which words failed and your test, and rewrites until its hour runs out
When a reading has refused this card's title and the refusal stands, the planning brief opens with it — the reading's words, the words that failed, and your test in the words the title reading's own brief uses, quoted from the one place — so the plan's title is written in your words the first time. When the plan lands, the planning record stays open while no reading of the new title has run; when the reading passes, the record is ended as today and Start opens; when it fails, the beat hands the reading back to the same session — the resume verb, with words that quote the new failure and say to rewrite the title and stem as the README says a writer does — on whatever machine the session runs, and the record follows the new session id. The hour is the record's, and a resume does not reset it. When the hour runs out with the title still failing, the session is stopped, the lane stays Planned with the reason on the card — the title failed the reading N times within the hour, and it is yours to rewrite — and Start opens by itself once a reading of your title passes, which is what the beat already does for a Planned lane. The readings cap applies per text as it does everywhere. ## Acceptance — behaviours

- With auto-fix on, a planning session that pushes its plan from a machine that is not the board's reaches Planned and starts without anyone levelling anything by hand, and never reads "finished its turn without a plan".
- A card whose suggestion's title failed a reading gets a plan whose title passes, or a card that says the hour ran out and what the reading said — never a card whose Start is closed by advice addressed to a session that has gone.
- No card the dial planned sits on your desk by the dial's own hand: `needle fixes all --stranded --count` prints 0 on the served board after the restart, once #453 is retitled.
- The suite, the ratchets, `tsc` and `vitest` are green before the fold, and the served board is rebuilt and restarted on the laptop after it.

## Rulings

1. **The dial keeps feeding the reading back until the hour runs out; then the card is his.** The owner, 2026-09-15, at this door: "ill go with your rec". Rejected: his at the first failure after the warning, because a rewrite is one cheap turn and a card on his desk is the slot wasted; the hour, which already bounds a planning session, bounds the rewrites too.
2. **The settle is measured, never clocked.** The session's reading of the suggestion's "settled against the corpus as it will be read". Rejected: a longer clock, because it waits the full loop on every plan and is wrong the first day the loop is slower.
3. **The beat levels the board's own copy itself; it does not wait for the five-minute loop.** Rejected: waiting for `level_trunks`, because that spends up to five minutes of a planning slot on every plan and ties the beat to a timer it does not own. The fetch runs once per turn's end, and the lane places it against the loop's lock the way the fold's `level_project` already is (card #123, item 3).
4. **A re-opened lane is Planned, never taken again.** `_ran` still holds the card; only the reason it is on his desk changes. Rejected: a new stage, because Planned already says what is true.
5. **A card whose hour ran out stays Planned, not Ended.** So his rewrite starts it on the next beat without him touching anything else — today's behaviour for a Planned lane whose Start opens. Rejected: Ended, because the beat does not open Start on an ended lane and he would have to click.
6. **A card whose writer is gone is his to retitle; no fresh session is opened to retitle a plan.** The search at this door found no verb that resumes a session gone from another machine's registry (`call` refuses across machines; a transcript resume reads this machine's files), and a brief that retitles an existing plan would be a new kind of session for one card. #453 is that card; the class fix is items 1 and 3, which keep the writer present.

## Deliberately not

- Changing the title test, who reads titles, when readings run, or the readings cap.
- A call that travels between machines (card #83, item 4).
- Changing the five-minute loop, or levelling any other machine's copy from the beat.
- A new stage on the fix lane, or a change to "took it once already".
- A session that retitles a plan whose writer is gone.
