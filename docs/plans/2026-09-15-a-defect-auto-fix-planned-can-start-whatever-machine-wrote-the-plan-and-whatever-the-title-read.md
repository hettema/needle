## Intent

When you turn auto-fix on, a verified defect gets a plan and a session without you, and the card moves with the work. Today the first card the switch took on Hello Revenue got its plan and then became yours with its Start closed: the plan was pushed from the rented machine, the board waited two minutes for it while it levels its own copy every five, called the session ended "without a plan", read the plan in minutes later, and by its own rule left the card yours from there; and the plan's title, written under the same words a cold reading had already refused on the suggestion, failed the reading again, so Start is closed on a card nobody will rewrite. What you lose: a slot of the four spent on a card that ends on your desk unstartable, with the reading's advice addressed to a writer who has gone, and a "fixing themselves" count that stalls while it looks busy.

After this plan, a planning session is judged on what reached the board, not on a clock: when its turn ends, the board levels its own copy of the project at once, whatever machine the session ran on, and reads the plan in; only a levelling that ran after the turn and found nothing ends the session. A plan that arrives after the board had already given up re-opens the card's work instead of leaving it yours. A planning session for a card whose title you could not place is told, at the top of its brief, which words failed and your test, so the plan's title is in your words the first time; and if a reading still refuses the new title, the board hands that reading back to the same session to rewrite, as many times as its hour allows. Only when the hour runs out with the title still failing is the card yours — with the reading's words on its face, and Start opening by itself the moment a reading of your title passes.

What does not change: the title test and who reads titles, the readings cap, the five-minute loop that levels every machine's copy, the number of fix lanes, and the rule that a card the dial took once is not taken again.

## Items

### 1. A planning session's turn is settled against what reached the board, not a clock
When a planning session's turn ends with no plan and no question on the card, the beat levels the board's own copy of the project at once — the one-project level the fold and the terminal use, one fetch per turn's end and never one per beat — whatever machine the session ran on, and the corpus is read in. The session is called ended without a plan only when a levelling that ran after the turn ended answered level: the trunk record's read time against the turn's end, never a number of seconds. A turn whose end the registry has not stamped yet is waited on as today, so the beat never fetches on a beat that has nothing to settle (ruling 3).

What counts as "a level ran and found nothing" is `TrunkState.level is True` and nothing else. `runtime/git.py::level` (331) answers `level = behind == 0` on a checkout it would not touch as readily as on one it fast-forwarded: a checkout merely off develop, or dirty and already level, answers True *and carries a note*; only a checkout that is behind and could not be moved answers False. So the beat reads `level`, never the note — keying off the note would stall every planning turn for the full hour on a board that is simply not on develop. A level that answered anything but True is not "nothing arrived": the beat writes it on the card once, in the state's own words (`note`, or "the level did not run"), and waits within the planning hour; when the hour then ends the session, the words say the level never ran rather than that the session wrote nothing, so a board whose checkout is dirty and behind is loud instead of silently costing an hour a card. The card's history names the level that settled it.

The same settle serves the corpus lanes (`_follow_corpus_lanes`, the second reader of the constant): a ruling and a split lane push to origin through the same `PUSH_LINE` the planning brief uses, so their turn-end clock rests on the same assumption and fails the same way. Done means: on the test board, a planning session whose plan is pushed to origin from a clone of the fixture's repository, leaving the board's own checkout behind and levelled by nothing else, is not ended — the beat's own level reads it in, the lane reaches Planned and the card starts; the same holds with the planning session placed on the floor's second machine, which is what "whatever machine" means; a session whose turn ends with nothing pushed is ended only after a level that ran after its turn answered level, and the card's history says so; a turn the registry has not stamped as ended yet is waited on and costs no fetch; a checkout dirty and behind leaves the lane planning with the level's own words on the card, once and not once a beat, and when the hour ends it the card says the level never ran; `test_a_planning_session_that_dies_ends_the_dials_part_and_the_card_says_why` holds unchanged; `PLANNING_SETTLE_SECONDS` has no reader left, the corpus lanes' settle included.
Hands out: search — every reader of `PLANNING_SETTLE_SECONDS`, `level_project`, `record_trunk`, `TrunkState.read_at` and `Session.machine` under `api/`, `board/`, `runtime/`, `infrastructure/` and the tests, with path and line; verifies each hit by reading it before changing it.
**Met:** `api/dial.py::_settled_against_the_trunk` levels the board's own copy at a turn's end and reads the corpus in; `PLANNING_SETTLE_SECONDS` is gone and both its readers — the planning settle and the corpus lanes' — use it. `tests/api/test_a_defect_auto_fix_planned_can_start.py` holds all four behaviours: a plan pushed to origin from a clone, with the board's checkout behind and nothing else levelling, reaches Planned and starts on the next beat; a session that pushed nothing is ended only after a level that answered level, with the words on the card; the fetch happens at a turn's end and not on a beat with a working session; and a checkout dirty and behind holds the lane with the level's own words, said once per spell, until the hour ends it saying the level never ran. The verdict is read and the note never is, so a board merely off develop does not stall.

### 2. A plan that arrives after the board gave up on its session re-opens the card's work
A fix lane the beat ended without a plan, whose card now carries a plan that carries its suggestion, goes back to Planned on the next beat, with a line on the card's history saying the plan arrived after the session was called ended, and Start is opened as for any plan the dial wrote. The card is still one the dial took once — it is not taken again — but it is not yours for the wrong reason. This reaches the cards already on your desk from this failure: Hello Revenue #453 re-opens on the first beat after the fold and the restart. The beat re-reads the machine before it judges such a lane: `_follow` reconciles when any lane stands at a live stage, because the doors a Start is judged by must be this read's, and an ended lane that can now reach Start is one more reason for the same read — so `FixStage.ENDED` joins that condition. (The card's link itself comes from the corpus sweep, not from the reconcile; what the reconcile buys is the door.)
Done means: on the test board, a lane ended without a plan whose plan lands afterwards reads Planned on the next beat, its history names the arrival, it starts when its Start is offered, and it is not planned a second time; `needle fixes` shows no ended lane on a card that carries a plan; on the served board after the restart, #453's lane reads Planned, its Start held by its title, which is yours to rewrite because its writer is gone (ruling 9), and Start opens by itself once a reading of your title passes.
**Met:** `api/dial.py::_follow_ended` takes a lane the beat ended before it was ever planned whose card now carries a plan back to Planned and opens Start, re-reading the machine first so the doors it is judged by are that beat's and not the ones answered when the card had no plan. That read is paid by the one card re-opening, not by the beat: ended lanes never go away, so triggering the beat's own reconcile on them — which is what this first shipped as — would have bought it on every beat for ever. Two tests hold it: a plan landing after the board gave up starts the card on the next beat, names the arrival on its history and is not planned a second time; and a lane that reached Planned and ran is never re-opened, whatever its card carries.

### 3. A planning session is told which words failed and your test, and rewrites until its hour runs out
When a reading has refused this card's title and the refusal stands, the planning brief opens with it — the reading's words, the words that failed, and your test in the words the title reading's own brief uses, quoted from the one place — so the plan's title is written in your words the first time. When the plan lands, the planning record stays open while no reading of the new title has run; when the reading passes, the record is ended as today and Start opens; when it fails, the beat hands the reading back to the same session — the resume verb, with words that quote the new failure and say to rewrite the title and stem as the README says a writer does — on whatever machine the session runs, and the record follows the new session id. The hour is the record's, and a resume does not reset it.

Two bounds this needs and did not have. **The number:** a planned lane whose Start is closed counts as held and against nothing, which is true while it is no process — and this item keeps its planning session alive. So a lane with an open planning record is never held: `held_lanes` takes one more reader and drops it, and the session counts against the number the whole time it is being handed readings (ruling 7). Without that, a number of four would hold four live planning sessions counting zero and open four fresh lanes beside them. **The face:** the reading judges the card's face, and on a card imported from the first board the face is never rewritten from its document (#150), so a rewrite of the plan's title cannot make that reading pass. The beat hands a reading back only while the card's title is its plan's; otherwise the lane stays Planned and the card is his at once, saying that its face does not show its plan's title (ruling 8).

The board remembers which reading it has already handed back on the fix lane's own note — the idiom `_start` uses to say a thing once — so a restart never hands the same refusal twice and the guard rests on no registry field. When the hour runs out with the title still failing, the session is stopped, the lane stays Planned with the reason on the card — the title failed the reading N times within the hour, and it is yours to rewrite — and Start opens by itself once a reading of your title passes, which is what the beat already does for a Planned lane. The readings cap applies per text as it does everywhere. Done means: on the test board, a verified defect whose title a cold reading refused gets a planning session whose brief opens with the refused words and the test (`tests/board/test_brief.py` grows this case beside the five rules); a plan that lands with a title that fails is read again, the session is resumed with the failure and the record follows the new id, and the lane still counts against the number; a second commit with a passing title starts the card; a title still failing when the hour has run out leaves the lane Planned, the session stopped, the card saying the hour ran out, and a rename by hand to a passing title starts it on the next beat; a session on the floor's second machine takes the same path; a card whose title passed on the first reading is untouched by all of this; a lane being handed readings counts against the number, so a board set to one opens no second lane beside it; and a card whose face does not show its plan's title is left to the owner at once, with that as the reason, rather than resumed for an hour it cannot win.

**Realigned during the build, 2026-09-15, and Met in the new shape.** This item said the writer's record stays open while the reading of its new title runs, that the beat resumes *that open session*, and that the hour is the record's. Building it showed the three cannot hold together: ruling 7 makes a lane with a live writer count against the number, and the reading that has to judge the title is a session too, so on a board set to one — the setting the owner runs — the writer held the only slot and the reading could never open. The card could not finish. A test demonstrated it before anything shipped.

What is built instead keeps every part of the intent and costs the deadlock nothing: **the board never opens a second session for a card while one of its own is working.** (A beat's width can still see both — the seat may open a reading of a title already in the corpus while the writer's turn-end stamp has not landed — and the cold read of 2026-09-15 named that; both count against the number, so the accounting stays honest either way.) When the plan lands the writer is stopped exactly as it was before, so the lane holds no process and the seat reads the title with room to spare. When a reading refuses that title, the beat resumes the writer *from its transcript* — the same colleague, its own plan in front of it — and opens its record for as long as it works; when its turn ends the record closes and the seat reads the title it now stands behind. The hour is therefore the fix lane's, measured from the moment the dial took the card (`_hours_up`), not a record's: being handed a third refusal buys no more time than the first, which is what ruling 1 asked of the record's hour and is now true of the card's whole life under the dial. `runtime/service.py::resume` brings a stopped session back from its transcript, so nothing new was needed for it.

**Met:** the planning brief opens with the refused words and the owner's test (`board/brief.py::refused_title_text`, quoted from `TITLE_TEST` and not paraphrased), held by a unit test and by the launch's own brief on the floor. The hand-back, the rewrite and the start are held end to end by `test_a_title_the_reading_refuses_goes_back_to_its_writer_until_a_reading_passes`: the plan lands, the seat reads and refuses, the next beat resumes the writer with a prompt quoting the refusal, the record follows the session that lives, the same refusal is never handed back twice, the writer's rewrite ends its record, and a passing reading starts the card. The hour case leaves the lane Planned with the card the owner's, and his own rewrite starts it on the next beat. The face case is held too: an imported card says once that its face does not show its plan's title and nothing is handed back.

7. **A live session is never counted as held, whatever its card's Start says.** The challenge round, 2026-09-15: held means *no process*, and item 3 keeps one. 8. **A reading is handed back only to a writer who can act on it.** The rewrite changes the plan's title; the reading judges the card's face; on an imported card those are not the same string (#150), so the hand-back would burn the hour and every reading in it.
