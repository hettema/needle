## Intent

A conversation starts from the board about nothing yet, and what it writes becomes a card. Lanes that run at the same time know about each other before they start, are told when they drift into each other's files while they run, and say what they touched before they fold, so a collision is either refused, seen, or written down — never silent. And no loop can ever start a lane: that is the owner's click, held by a test.

### 1. An idea door in the head
As its suggestion says. Done means: Idea in the head of every project's board opens a conversation in that project's checkout on the rule's slot and model, with the brief the suggestion specifies and an optional first line typed into the door; listed on the attention rail as in discussion; never hands on a tree; a document it writes becomes a card whose history says it was born from a conversation.

### 2. Lanes that run together know about each other
As its suggestion says. Done means: every lane's brief lists the other live lanes on the project with their footprints and one line each; the board recomputes footprints from live worktrees' actual diffs on every read and marks drifted lanes as colliding on both cards and the rail; a watercooler per project that every lane reads at start and before the fold and appends to when it touches a seam, its last line shown on each live card; a fold that rebased over another live lane's edits says so on the card; the two-lane fixture test.

### 3. Start is the owner's click, held by a ratchet
From the gap list. Done means: a ratchet under `tests/ratchets/` proves the runtime's start is reached from the doors module only and never from the loops module, in the shape the gap list describes; the gap list's suggestion gains a line saying which of its four items this closed.

## Acceptance criteria
1. Idea opens a window with the brief; a plan the session writes is a card within seconds with the conversation in its history.
2. Two fixture lanes with overlapping actual edits are marked colliding on the next read; each lane's brief names the other; a watercooler line from one appears on the other's card; a fold over the other's edits is named on the card.
3. The ratchet fails on a call to the runtime's start from the loops module.

## Rulings
Recorded as the build makes them, each with the alternative rejected.

1. **The watercooler lives in the board's store, not in a file in the project.** A file under `docs/` in a lane's worktree is invisible to every other lane until the fold — which is exactly when a line is too late — and a file in the main checkout would be uncommitted work that stops the trunk sync (`git.level` refuses a dirty checkout). So the watercooler is a table (`watercooler`: project, card, actor, at, text), written through `needle watercooler SLUG N "…"` the way rows are written through `needle row`, read in every lane's brief and by `needle fold` before the push, and shown as its last line on every card with hands on it. The machine-level discussion folder stays what it is: a channel for sessions on this laptop, not a board thing. Rejected: `docs/watercooler.md`, for the two reasons above.
2. **A lane is told about its neighbours by card, sentence and both footprints.** The brief lists every other lane with hands on the project — its card number and title, the lane's own sentence (working, asking, stopped), what its worktree has actually changed (`Touching:`), and what its plan names (`Its plan names:`) — followed by the rule: leave those files to their lanes unless your plan names them; say so in the watercooler first if you must; the board marks two lanes in one file as colliding on both cards. The footprints are read from git on every read of the machine (the lane loop already read them for the collision pill; now once, for the lanes, the drift and the doors together). Rejected: telling a lane only the other lanes' declared footprints — a lane whose plan named nothing is exactly the one that drifts.
3. **Drift is named on both sides, and the fold names what it landed over.** `board/collision.py::drift` maps each live lane to the other live lanes editing a file it is also editing; the sentence names every one ("#241's lane is also editing engine/metering.py."). At the fold, `needle fold` reads the machine, prints the watercooler, prints every live lane whose edits this fold lands over, pushes, and then writes "Folded over #N's edits in <files>" on the folding card, "#M folded over this lane's edits in <files>; re-verify them at the fold" on the other, and the same line on the watercooler as the board's own — so the other lane hears it in its next brief and at its own fold. The fold does not refuse: a lane that folds over another's edits was allowed to (the owner's override, or a plan naming the same ground); it is the silence that was the defect.
4. **A conversation's document names the conversation, and the board says the rest.** The Idea door chooses the session id before the window opens and tells the session to head its document with `**Found by:** the owner, from the board's Idea door on <date> (conversation <short id>)`. A birth whose `Found by` names a conversation the board itself recorded gets "Born from a conversation on <date> (<short> on <slot>, from the Idea door)" on its history row; a short id the board never opened is not claimed. Rejected: inferring the birth from timing (a document landing while an idea session lives) — a hand-written file at the same moment would be misattributed, and a claim that is sometimes wrong is worse than one that is sometimes absent. Rejected: a machine-only head key — `Found by` is the corpus's own convention for where a document came from.
5. **An idea is a discussion about no card.** The `discussions` table's card becomes nullable (migration 0005) rather than a second table: the one list tells a conversation from hands on a tree by its session id either way, and the rail counts both kinds as "in discussion". The Idea session runs in the project's main checkout, commits its document on develop and pushes it — the main checkout is for reading, docs and the sync, and a document is docs.
6. **The ratchet allows two pressed modules, not one.** The gap list said `api/doors.py` only; `api/runtime_cli.py` holds `needle start`, the owner's own terminal verb, which is his click as much as the button is. The ratchet refuses the runtime's `start` anywhere else under `api/` and refuses the loops importing the doors. Ratcheted at the altitude of the intent (a person presses), not the method (one file).
7. **Sequenced after 06, built before it.** The plan said "after 06"; the owner launched this card first. Nothing here depends on 06's pinned head or defects rail: the Idea door sits in the existing head's tools, the colliding and in-discussion counts on the existing attention line, and the marks on the existing card faces. 06 will find them there.
