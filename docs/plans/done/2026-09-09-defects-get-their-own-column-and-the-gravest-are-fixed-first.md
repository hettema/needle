## Intent

The owner scans the defects the way he scans every other stage of the work: as a column he can open and close, that says at its head how many are his, how many nobody has read yet, how many wait on a signal and how many are fixing themselves, and that lists them gravest first. Today the defects are a strip folded into Backlog that shows one number, holds its cards in the order they were filed, and cannot be opened without hiding every idea; and nothing on the board says how bad any defect is, so "fix everything" is the only order auto-fix knows, oldest first, while the queue outgrows the night.

Gravity is a judgment, and the board already has a seat that judges every defect once, independently, from its document and its source: the reading that verifies who fixes it (#59) and, since #74, whether its title can be placed. That reading answers one more question here, from the document it already holds (on Needle the paragraph every defect carries, the intent it breaks and what the owner loses while it does; on the other projects, whose defects carry no such paragraph, the document whole): who the defect reaches, how it shows and what it costs, on a scale in his words, and how often it bites. The column is ordered by that grade and auto-fix takes the gravest verified defect first instead of the oldest. A grade is a claim with a fingerprint like every reading: the words say what in the document selected it, a changed document is read again, and a reader of another make audits the first thirty.

What does not change: who ranks, who starts, and what the mark means. The grade never opens auto-fix wider than the mark and the reading do; a defect graded gravest and marked his stays his. Backlog becomes ideas only, which is what its birth ruling wanted ("Backlog should be for ideas in my mind, easier to scan that way", #10).

### 2. The reading that verifies a defect's mark also grades it, in the owner's words

## Rulings

1. **A column, not a page.** The owner, 2026-09-09: "I don't mind an extra column because I can collapse them and see the board in the way I want to see it." #10's ruling rejected a column for width and said a column is one definition away if he still wants it; this is that definition, and the laptop's width is paid by starting furled, as the archive columns do. Rejected: the page in the Triage page's shape this conversation proposed, because he reads the board as columns and a second surface is a second way to look.
2. **The scale is three parts in his words, and it is every project's.** Who it reaches (a client or the public, money, you, a session); what breaks (lies, loses, costs, looks); how often (every time, sometimes, once seen). The parts are the doctrine's own discriminators, which every project on the board adopts: §1's external exposure past a bound is the reach; §6 (a partial result reported as whole is bad information) and §11 (or the board lies) make *lies* the gravest kind; §5's "silent or late" is why *lies* outranks *loses*, which is loud. Read at the door against 23 live defects on Hello Revenue and the laptop, none of which carries Needle's "intent it breaks" paragraph: twenty graded cleanly from the document alone; two — a client able to pick a personal ad account by mistake, a spend clickable where it should not be — were undersold by what-breaks alone and are exactly what the reach part lifts; one was an idea in a defect's clothing, which the reading says. Rejected: a number the reading lands, because a number carries no reason a cold audit can check and two readers' P1s mean two things; free text, because it cannot be sorted. The owner reads P0 to P4 at a glance: the bands in item 3 are that ladder, computed from the parts and never landed, and the face may name a band that way if he says so on this card; the parts stay the reason under it either way. Proposed at the door; the owner amends the words on this card and nothing else needs to change.
3. **One reader.** The grade is a field on the reading that verifies the mark, never a second reading of the same defect: #74 set the precedent (the title's verdict rides on the mark's reading), and #87 holds one reader per kind. Rejected: a gravity reading of its own, which would double the readings on every defect for one more field.
4. **The order is the machine's and the owner does not rank it.** Band, then who it reaches, then how often, then age; unread last. Rejected: reach first as a strict order, because an ugly sentence on a client's page would then outrank a test that lies to every session; and what-breaks first as a strict order, because a client able to misuse an account would then sit under every log line that misleads a session. The band takes the doctrine's two cuts in the order it makes them: harm outside first, then false before lost. Rejected: unread first, so he sees the reading is behind — the head line's count says that, and an unread defect on top of a sorted column is a defect nothing has judged sitting above ones something has.
5. **Readings run with auto-fix off.** The dial is his ruling about what enters execution without him; a reading enters nothing. Rejected: a second switch for readings, because a switch for something that never acts is a switch he would never touch.
6. **Impact against the focus is #87's, not this card's.** This grade says how bad; #87's reading says whether a card moves what matters now. When #87 lands its lens orders this column like every other, and gravity stays the column's own order.

## Deliberately not

- Grading ideas. An idea breaks nothing; #87 reads what it moves.
- Sorting the readings queue by gravity. Nothing unread has a grade.
- Fixing Needle's own defects while other work is live. That is #43.
- A gravity a session sets by hand on the document. The document says what breaks; the grade is the second reading's, so the writer of a defect never grades its own.
