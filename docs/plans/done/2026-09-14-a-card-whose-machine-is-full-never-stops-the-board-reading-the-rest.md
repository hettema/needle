## Intent — what this achieves and why

The board reads one card at a time and never lets one card hold the rest (card #138's intent; card #83's plan, item 4: the rented machine takes the cards the laptop cannot hold). Today a card whose own machine is full holds every other card on the board: since 16:06Z on 2026-09-14 every beat picked Omarchy #3, the oldest unread title, asked the full laptop for a reading, was refused, wrote the refusal on the card and read nothing else, twenty-four beats in a row, while 67 Hello Revenue and 60 Needle defects that the rented machine reads with 25 GB free waited behind it. The owner's question of the hour — whether auto-fix can go back on — waits on that grading.

What this plan makes true: a beat opens the oldest reading that *can* open this beat. A card whose machine has no room is left out of that beat's choice and says so on its face once, not once a minute; when every machine is full the beat opens nothing, as today. The head already shows each machine's room by name (card #83), so a count of held cards per machine is not built here: the card's own note says why it waits, and the machine's line says why the machine cannot take it. Rejected: moving a ground project's cards to another machine — a machine's own record runs nowhere else, by the rule this plan reuses.

## Terrain — where to look, not what to write

- **The beat's choice.** `api/dial.py::_take_next` (356-462): builds `candidates` (verified defects, boards with the switch on) and `unread` (defects, titles, parked cards) per project, sorts `unread` by age and hands the first to `_triage`, then returns — one act per beat (card #100). `_full` (341-354) opens nothing only when *no* machine has room, so a full laptop beside a rented machine with room is "not full" and the beat runs.
- **The open, and the refusal.** `api/dial.py::_triage` (585-662): builds the brief, asks `self.runtime.start_windowless(WindowlessStart(repo=project.path, …))` (622-629); when the launch is not alive it writes `The board could not start a reading of {of_what}: {launch.reason}` on the card as a DIAL note (632-635) and returns. Nothing takes the next candidate and nothing remembers the refusal, so the same card is chosen sixty seconds later.
- **The rule that knows.** `runtime/service.py::place` (846-852) → `domain/machine.py::choose_machine` (134-180): for a project that is a machine's own record it returns `(None, "<machine> is the machine this project records and it is full (…); its cards run nowhere else")` when that machine's room is full — the very sentence the refusal carries. `find_ground` (128) says which project is which machine's record. The rooms of this pass: `api/loops.py::headroom_now` reads `self.runtime.rooms(...)` into `self._rooms` (985) on every pass; `runtime.rooms()` reads them now. `MachineRoom` (`domain/machine.py:94`) and `Headroom` (`domain/dial.py:198`, `full`, `sentence`).
- **What a card remembers.** A card's history is the `audit` table (`AuditKind.DIAL`, `Actor.MACHINE`); `self.live.note` writes it; the store reads a card's rows back (`infrastructure/store.py`, the history the card face shows). ## Items

1. **The beat leaves out a card its machine cannot open.** Before the seat chooses, each unread candidate's project is asked the same question the launch would ask — can this project's machine open a reading now, over the rooms read this pass — and one whose answer is no is left out of this beat's choice, so the oldest card that *can* open is the one opened. Done means: with the laptop full and the rented machine with room, a beat opens a reading of the oldest card whose project the rented machine reads, and Omarchy's cards wait without holding anyone; with every machine full the beat opens nothing and the head says so, as today.
**Met:** `api/dial.py::_openable` asks each unread card's project the launch's own question — `runtime.place` over the rooms the pass read into the head (`Live.machine.machines`) — once per project per beat, and the seat sorts and opens among the cards whose machine answered; a full board still returns at `_full()` before any choice. `tests/api/test_a_card_whose_machine_is_full_never_stops_the_board_reading_the_rest.py::test_a_beat_opens_the_oldest_card_a_machine_with_room_can_read_and_the_rest_wait` lays the laptop full as the ground of a project whose cards are a day older and the rented machine with 24 GB: the beat opens the fixture project's oldest card on the rented machine, the ground's cards carry no launch refusal, and with the rented machine full too the beat opens nothing and the head names its numbers. Extended in the lane after the independent review (call 135, session 5ca8b465) traced a refusal the rooms do not carry — a ground whose machine did not answer, no subscription with allowance — that still held the beat: `_triage` answers the launch's refusal, the beat passes that project over for the rest of the beat and opens the next project's oldest; `test_a_launch_refused_for_another_cause_holds_the_beat_no_more_than_room_does` refuses every start in the ground project and shows five beats each open the fixture project's next card on the rented machine. The carried suggestion names this class in its own Fix line.

2. **A card left out says so once per spell.** The refusal is written on the card when it is first left out for room and again when its reading finally opens, never on every beat in between; how the seat remembers is the lane's call, and the card's own last DIAL row is already readable through the store. Done means: a card left out on twenty consecutive beats carries one note saying its machine is full, then one saying the reading opened; the count of identical refusals on one card in one hour is at most one.
**Met:** `api/dial.py::_left_out` writes `board/dial.py::left_out_words` on the card unless its last dial note by the machine is already one (`said_left_out`, a prefix comparison since the machine's numbers move between beats); the store's `history` is the memory, so a restart reads the same row. The same module's `test_a_card_left_out_says_so_once_per_spell_and_once_more_when_its_reading_opens` runs twenty beats, each opening and landing another card, and the left-out card carries one note; the laptop freed, the next beat opens its reading and its history reads started, left out, in that order. The launch's own refusal is written through the same reader (`_say_once` over the `REFUSED` prefix): the third test's five refused beats leave one line.

3. **The change is held by tests, not by memory.** A test opens a beat with two machines, one full holding a ground project's card and one with room holding another project's older-or-younger card, and shows the beat opens the second; a second test shows the note is written once across many beats. Done means: reverting item 1 fails the first test naming the card that should have opened; reverting item 2 fails the second on the count.
