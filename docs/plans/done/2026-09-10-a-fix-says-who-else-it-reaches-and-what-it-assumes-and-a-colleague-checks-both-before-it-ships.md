## Intent

A review ends when the work holds, and the fixes a review makes are held to the same bar as the build. Today a fix is scoped by the finding a reader showed and stands on whatever its writer assumed about the rest of the system, and the only thing that catches a fix that missed its sibling, broke another reader of the same surface, or stood on a wrong premise is the next full pass, an hour later. On Hello Revenue #456 that turned a two-hour build into thirty-six hours of review: by the closing session's reading of the record, thirteen of thirty-four passes found a defect the previous rounds' own repairs had made, and twelve of those were findable at fix time by a search over the tree or by driving the real path. The tests a fix brought passed with it, because a fix's author writes them from the fix's own premise.

After this card, every fix line in a review record says who else the fix reaches and what it assumes; a colleague of the other kind, reached through the board's call so the call is a row and not a launch, tries to break exactly those two claims before the round ships, and the record quotes its verdict with the call it came from; every claim the reader broke has a disposition; a finding caused by an earlier fix says so on its line, so the board can count how often a review chases its own repairs and whether the reader caught it first; and a card whose record skipped any of this cannot close, in any project on the board. The rule itself is already written — §13 says a repair names its dependencies and a cold reader asks who else calls this — and is held by nothing; this card makes it held. What the owner gets: fewer review rounds, because the fix that would have cost the next pass is caught in the minutes after it is written, and a number on the fifth Hello Revenue close after this ships that says whether that happened.

What does not change: no cap on passes — the loop still ends by §13's rule of 2026-09-09, at whatever count the work earns; records dated on or before the fold's day are history and are not rewritten; Hello Revenue's `hr-feature-review` skill points writers at the reviews README for the record's shape, so the new grammar reaches them without an edit there.

## Rulings

1. **The colleague's verdict is a refusal at the close, not a note the next pass may read.** The owner left this call to the plan. Rejected: the suggestion's first form, a quoted verdict with the five-close loop deciding later whether it should block — §5 says a boundary whose failure is silent or late gets a mechanism, and this one fails both ways: a round shipped unread looks exactly like a round read, and the register's §13 line has claimed since 2026-09-05 that the repair rule is read by the next pass, which #456 shows it was not. What the door holds is exactly this: a card cannot close without the evidence that every round's repairs were read cold and every broken claim was answered. It does not hold that an unread round never reached the trunk — the fold comes before the close, and a lane that skipped the read finds out at the close, with its code already landed; the register says so in those words. The cost is one colleague call per round, which §13 has asked for since 2026-09-09; what is new is that skipping it now costs the close.
2. **The refusal sits at the close, where the record is already read, and so holds every project's records.** `api/doors.py::close` already refuses a code lane without its record; the same door validates the record it is given. Rejected: a refusal at the fold — the fold knows no record path, a fold without a close already lands the card on the owner's attention rather than on shipped (§14), and a second place that judges the record is a second way. Rejected: a ratchet alone — it would hold Needle's records and nobody else's, and the pattern that prompted this card is Hello Revenue's. Two things the door does not do today and must: it validates a record whenever one is supplied — today a docs-only lane, or a card with no known lane, returns before looking at the file it was named — while a docs-only close may still omit one; and it reads the record from the project's own tree through the runtime's lane-document path, so a lane standing on another machine is read where it stands, and an absolute path outside the project, which the door accepts today, is refused. The record's `**Plan:**` stem must be the card's plan.
3. **The record's date decides, strictly after the fold's day.** A record whose filename date is on or before the day this card folds is exempt, whatever passes it runs afterwards: the lanes in flight at the fold wrote their early rounds under the old form, and refusing them at close would be a rule applied backwards. A same-day record cannot say from its name whether it began before or after the fold, so the fold's day is exempt whole. A record whose filename carries no date is not the README's shape and is refused on that alone. Hello Revenue's five judged closes are therefore lanes whose record began after that day — the clean measure, at the price of a slower signal.
4. **The grammar has one source and one reader, and the reader is repaired where it is wrong.** The form of the fix line, the verdict line and the repair-caused mark lives in Needle's `docs/reviews/README.md`, the file that already holds the class rule; Hello Revenue's and the machine's reviews READMEs carry the effective template with its citation, as Hello Revenue's does for the class rule today — both omit the `## The passes` section and the `**Plan:**` line the readers need, and are brought level. The one reader is `board/parse.py::review_of`, which already reads passes and dispositions onto the card; the door, the card's progress line, the ratchets and the loop's reading all read through it. Three things it does today that the new forms cannot stand on, all inside this change: it counts an indented numbered finding under a pass as a pass (Hello Revenue's campaign record of 2026-09-08 reads 157 passes and has 22); it reads the word FIXED anywhere in an entry as the disposition, so "NOT FIXED IN THE LANE … filed" counts as fixed; and the class ratchet finds dispositions with its own walk, which disagrees with the reader on fenced examples and headings. The pass counter reads top-level leads only; the fate is read from the disposition's own token at the line's tail; the class ratchet keeps its predicate and takes its dispositions from the shared reader.
5. **The reader falsifies the lines, not the diff, and its verdict is a row before it is a quote.** The completeness read of a round is handed the round's FIXED lines and has one job per line: re-run the search behind the reach and name a sibling or a reader the line left out; drive the premise and say what it saw. Rejected: a fourth full pass per round — a full pass is what costs the hour, and its first finding on #456 was the repair thirteen times because nobody asked the narrow question. The reader is of the other kind and is reached through `needle call` and `wait` (§12) — a call is a row the board reads, a bare `codex exec` is not — and the verdict line in the record names the call's number and the commit it read, so the door can check the row: the call exists, its colleague is of the other kind, and its answer landed after the call. A verdict typed without a call behind it is exactly the trace §5 warns of, and the door refuses it. Every claim the reader broke gets a disposition after it — fixed, with the fix's own line, or a record-only correction the writer makes and re-reads alone, which §13 permits — and the door refuses a broken claim with no disposition. Rejected: "the last verdict says complete" as the test — it would refuse a finish §13 allows (a reader's last words about the record's wording, corrected by the writer) and accept an earlier break left unanswered by a later, unrelated round.
6. **The doctrine words are the owner's, ruled at this door.** The suggestion's `Fix:` line said so. For §13, replacing the clause "and the affected dependencies; it changes enough to remove the cause and reconcile those dependencies": *"and the affected dependencies — who else the fix reaches and what it assumes about who else reads or writes the thing, written on the fix's own line; it changes enough to remove the cause and reconcile those dependencies"*. For the cold reader's clause, replacing "who else calls this, what is the sibling case, what list was written by hand": *"who reads the fix's line and tries to break it — who else calls this, what is the sibling case, what list was written by hand, what does the fix assume and what drove it — and whose verdict is on the record before the round ships"*. Two sentences change and none is added; the record-only correction §13 already permits stands, and ruling 5's contract is written to honour it. Ruled 2026-09-10 at this door: "Wording change looks fine to me. Let's try it." — the words stand as written, and the lane commits the edit naming #110 (the doctrine hook, `hooks/commit-msg`). The same words set the loop's cadence to daily: "lets let the loop check every day. We develop a lot, I wouldn't be surprised if we cross the 5 today already."
7. **A cold reader needs a call that can ask for a fresh colleague, so the call's recency defect is carried here.** `needle call codex` resumes the most recently changed Codex worker (`runtime/codex.py::warm`), which as this is written is #109's live lane; the defect filed 2026-09-07 says a call must land on a colleague fit to answer, or say which session it picked. A cold read of a round wants no earlier context at all, so the call gains the one thing the defect asks for and this card needs: a way to name a fresh colleague of the other kind, at the effort judgment gets, in a sandbox that can run a read-only probe, with the row recorded as any call is. Rejected: sequencing this card after that defect — it has waited three days unstarted and the owner ranked this card first; the fix is one item here and the defect's card folds under this one.
8. **The loop counts one event in one unit, at two stages.** The event is a finding whose line carries the repair-caused mark, counted once — a break the reader named and the next pass reports again is one event, the pass's line citing the verdict's address. The stage is where the mark first appears: *caught*, on a line of a cold reader's verdict, before the round shipped; *escaped*, on a line of a full pass. The five judged closes are the first five Hello Revenue cards closed after this card's fold whose REVIEW row names a record dated after the fold's day, in the order of their close rows — the board's rows, not the folder, because a filename says neither when a record closed nor whether it is finished. The baseline is written in the same unit before the fold (item 3). Rejected: counting passes whose opening finding is a repair — one pass can hold several such findings, the thirteen on #456 were not all of the immediately preceding round's repair, and a count of passes cannot be compared to a bar set in findings. *Refined at the review (pass two's cold reader, 2026-09-10):* the unit is the finding, not the repair's address — two findings a full pass makes on one repair are two escapes, as the baseline below counts them — and a finding is caught when a verdict under its pass or an earlier one broke the address it cites, escaped otherwise; "counted once" holds for a break the reader named and the next pass reports again, which is one finding.
