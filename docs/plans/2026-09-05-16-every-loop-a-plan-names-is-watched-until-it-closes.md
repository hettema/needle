## Intent

A plan says what it will change and what would show it worked; that is the thesis the shared doctrine already asks for in prose ("we think X will change Y because Z; if we see A, then B"). After this plan the observable A is one line the board can execute, `Loop: <what> — <kind> <target> [expect <value>] by <date> [every <N>]`, in the WATCH grammar the board already reads, one per thesis. The card shows a plan's loops before the lane starts. At the close, every named loop becomes a WATCH row the board reads on its cadence, written from the plan itself, and a close that would leave a named loop with no readable signal refuses in the voice it already uses for a WATCH row that names none. A WAITS row can no longer stand in for a loop the plan named. Two verbs give the signals the parked loops never had: how many lanes have folded since a date, and what every closed card's HANDED OUT row says, across cards. The machine's card 12 gets its two parked loops as WATCH rows the day those verbs exist.

What does not change: the WATCH grammar and its readers; the close-out stance grammar (08, 13); the rule that the board reads repositories and never writes into them; what a loop is — the doctrine's thesis, not a check-up.

### 1. The grammar: a plan names its loops in the WATCH grammar
`docs/plans/README.md` (the source) says, beside the handout paragraph: a plan whose change is a bet ends its `## Loop` (or the item the loop judges) with one `Loop:` line per thesis, `Loop: <what will be observed> — url|file|command|owner <target> [expect <value>] by <YYYY-MM-DD> [every <N>h|<N>d]`, the WATCH row's grammar verbatim so the close can copy it. Why, in two lines: a loop written only in prose is closed by whoever remembers it, and the session closing a card is the one under the most pressure to forget; a loop written as a signal is closed by the board. The reader in `board/parse.py` reads `Loop:` lines the way `handouts_of` reads `Hands out:` (head of line or sentence boundary, never a mention in prose, the sentence to the end of its paragraph), attributed to the item or section they end, parsed through `parse_watch`; the card shows them and the brief prints them beside the handouts. Done means: a fixture plan with two `Loop:` lines shows both on its card and in its brief; a `Loop:` line `parse_watch` cannot read is a verdict line on the card in the voice of an unreadable gate; a plan with none shows nothing new; the three real corpora read without error, with the count of loops per corpus in the close-out. Hands out: `execution` runs the reader over the three corpora and tables the counts; verifies by opening two plans the table says carry loops and one it says does not.

### 2. The close writes a WATCH row per named loop, and refuses a loop with no signal
`needle close` reads the card's plan for its `Loop:` lines and writes one WATCH row per loop from the plan's own text; `--watch` remains for a signal the plan did not name and is required only when the plan named none. A named loop whose line does not read refuses the close naming it, in the voice of "The WATCH row names no signal"; a WAITS row is never a stance on a named loop. The board reads every WATCH row on the card on its cadence, as it reads the one today, and the card moves to Done when all of them deliver, shows overdue when any passes its date with nothing delivered, and asks the owner for the owner-kind ones at their due time. Done means: on the door fixture, a plan with two loops closes with two WATCH rows and no `--watch`; a plan with none refuses without `--watch` as today; a plan with one unreadable `Loop:` line refuses naming the line; a card with two WATCH rows reads both, delivers on both, and is overdue on either; `tests/api/test_doors.py` and the loop tests carry each case.

### 3. Two verbs so a loop always has a command
`needle folds [project] --since <date>` prints the lanes folded since the date — card, fold commit, day — and the count, read from the store's fold facts (the same facts the fold writes to the card today). `needle handouts [project]` tables HANDED OUT rows across Executed and Done cards: per role, named against dispatched, and the kinds dispatched and never named with their counts and the cards they came from. Both print plain text a WATCH command can `expect` against and run under a second on the real store. Done means: `needle folds hellorevenue --since 2026-09-04` counts the lanes the board folded since that day and the count matches the cards in Executed and Done whose fold is dated after it; `needle handouts hellorevenue` shows #403's row as general-purpose ×5 unnamed; both verbs have tests on the fixture store; `needle --help` lists them. Hands out: `search` — every place the store records a fold's day and commit (`board/`, `runtime/`, `domain/`), so the verb reads the fact the board already writes and does not invent a second record; verifies by reading the one file the search names as the writer.

### 4. The parked loops get their rows
With the verbs live, the machine's card 12 gets two WATCH rows through `needle row omarchy 12 WATCH …`: the twenty folds with the role files live (`command needle folds --since 2026-09-04 expect >= 20 by 2026-09-26 every 1d`), and the unnamed dispatch that names the next role (`command needle handouts expect <a kind dispatched and never named on five or more cards> by 2026-09-19 every 1d`, spelled as the verb's output allows). The WAITS row on card 12 keeps what is genuinely a ruling (item 4b) and loses the two loops. Done means: card 12 shows the two rows and the board reads them on their cadence; the redo-rate half of the `search` loop (`machine burn`'s verification-against-redo count) is named on the row's `what` and read by the session the row wakes, because the board runs `needle` verbs and `machine burn` is the machine's — written as such, not hidden. Hands out: nothing; the rows are a judgment about which loops matter.

## Loop
We think writing a loop as a signal the board executes will change whether stated loops close, because the close will then refuse to park one and the board, not a session's memory, reads it. ## Rulings
1. **The close writes the rows from the plan, the session does not retype them.** Rejected: requiring a `--watch` per loop — the session under close pressure is the failure this plan ends, and retyping a line the plan already carries is the form the doctrine refuses.
2. **The WATCH grammar, verbatim, never a second grammar for loops.** Rejected: a `Loop:` line in prose read by a new parser — one grammar for "what will be observed" or the two drift, and `parse_watch` already exists.
3. **The verbs are Needle's; the redo rate stays the machine's.** `machine burn` reads transcripts the board does not hold; a WATCH row that needs it names it in its `what` for the session the row wakes. Rejected: the board shelling out to `machine burn` — the board never runs anything on the machine but `needle` (its own layers ratchet).
