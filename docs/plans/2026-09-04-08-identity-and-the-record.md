## Intent

A card's identity survives anything a person does to its document, and the board's record is readable by the projects it serves: a plan that changes its name keeps its card and its history; a project's close-out check is one grammar in one place, held for every project on the board; and what sessions wrote on cards can be read back by a project's own tooling, so nothing that used to live in a card file is lost to a note that reads git.

### 1. A card follows its document: through a rename, a new title, and the close
As its suggestion says, and the two carried ones with it. Done means: a document that disappears in the same read as one appears is a rename when git says so or the bodies match closely; the card keeps its number and gains "renamed from <old path>"; no new card is born; a card's title is the title of the document it cites, on every effect that changes the link — born, renamed, relinked, carried — with the birth title kept in the history, so a card carried by a plan reads the plan's title and not the suggestion's (on 2026-09-05 #63 read "every rung is a Claude one, so Sol is never given the wheel", the opposite of its plan, and #30 read its carried suggestion's title); a card's citation is shown only when the corpus holds the file, and follows it through archive, rename and `git mv`, so a finished card names its plan once, where the plan is; tests: a renamed suggestion changing stem and title finds one card with the new document and the old history; a suggestion's card carried by a plan reads the plan's title and keeps its number; an archived plan's card cites the archived path only. Card #11 on Needle's board, whose document was renamed into #18's on 2026-09-04 and which now sits in Not now with no document, is retired into #18 (shipped) saying so on both — what this plan first asked as a merge, before #18 shipped under the new name. The omarchy board's #13/#15 (plan `2026-09-04-10-spotify-is-the-web-player` renamed to `…-spotify-that-actually-sounds-good`, stem and title both, in `01adff8`) is a second instance of the same pair: merge and retire it the same way, #13 into #15, which card 16 there already closed.

### 3. The record is readable by the projects it serves
From the gap list, item 3. Done means: a read-only API and a `needle` verb give any project's tooling every row ever written on its cards (DELIVERED, WATCH, REVIEW, RULING and the rest) with the card, the time and the writer, as JSON; Hello Revenue's morning note can read its DELIVERED sentences again from it instead of from a card file that no longer exists, and the note's reader docstring that records the loss is updated by this lane with a commit on that repository's develop when its tree is clean, otherwise the exact edit goes in the close-out.
**Met:** migration 0013 puts `written_at` and `writer` on every row (backfilled from the audit: `tests/infrastructure/test_record.py` rounds a store down to 0012 and back and reads the same record); `Store.rows_written`, `GET /api/projects/{slug}/rows?since=` and `needle rows SLUG [--since] [--kind]` give every row on every card as JSON with the card, its title and column, the time and the writer (`tests/api/test_api.py::test_the_record_is_read_back_as_rows_with_time_and_writer`, `tests/api/test_cli.py::test_rows_prints_the_record_as_json_with_time_and_writer`). ## Acceptance criteria
1. Rename a suggestion changing both stem and title: one card, new document, old history, no new card.
2. `needle close` refuses an Executed close whose archived plan has an unstanced promise, naming the promise, on a fixture project.
3. `needle rows <project>` (or the API) returns every row on every card as JSON; the morning note's reader in Hello Revenue reads from it, or its exact edit is in the close-out.
4. Cards #11 and #18 are one card on Needle's board with the merged history.

## Rulings
Recorded as the build makes them, each with the alternative rejected.

1. **A card imported from 0.1 keeps its own title; a card born from the corpus follows its document's title on every read.** The imported title was the owner's words for the card, which no document ever held: 74 of Hello Revenue's 132 imported cards differ from their plan's title, and those plans are the older, mechanism-named ones (a plan titled for its hardening mechanism against the owner's title naming the client and the outcome). Rejected: retitling every card, which would have put the mechanism back on the face the owner ranks from. Also: the face follows an in-place title edit, not only a link change, because the owner's bar is the document's current title.
2. **A vanished document is a rename by title first, then by git's own record, then by an identical body against the previous read.** "Closely" is the Found-by line and the whole intent body identical — stricter than the suggestion's first paragraph, and nothing a session has to defend. Rejected: a similarity ratio with a threshold (git already has one, and it is git's word that is read); a stored body signature on the card (a migration, and stale after every edit). Git is asked only when something appeared in the corpus that could be the answer, so a card whose document is gone for good (Hello Revenue's #120) costs nothing per read.
3. **A duplicate card is retired by quoting its history on the survivor, never by re-homing its lines.** `placements` reads each number's last move by id, and omarchy's #13 moved to Not now after #15 reached Done: a re-homed line would have made the board say the owner placed #15 in Not now. Rejected: re-homing (found in review pass 2); leaving the rows behind (their text would survive only cut at 140 characters in the audit) — the rows move, and a one-per-card kind the survivor already has is quoted whole.
4. **The stance grammar is Needle's, and the dependency runs Needle → the projects.** One vocabulary (met with evidence, deviated with a pointer), at the item, read by `board/parse.py` and counted on every running lane (plan 13); Needle's close holds it for every project. Hello Revenue re-spells it rather than importing, for the reason its own file records for the handouts sentence: its archive gate reads hermetically in CI and at a commit, where Needle is not installed. Rejected: Hello Revenue importing Needle; Needle reading Hello Revenue's `## Close-out` block over acceptance criteria (a second parser in the one place, over a list Needle's own plans never stance). Left as it stands, and named for the owner: Hello Revenue's archive gate still stances acceptance criteria in a block of its own, a method he and a colleague ruled on 2026-09-01; two written records now select different promise sets for that project, and whether one list should go is his.
5. **Time and writer are columns on the row, and the record is the standing rows.** Rejected: a join on the audit at read time (its row line is written for a person: cut at 140 characters, kind and verb in prose); a second log table of every write (a copy of every row). A one-per-card row rewritten in place carries the time of its standing text; the earlier text is on the card's history, where plan 04 put it.
6. **The morning note is not rewired.** It runs on GitHub Actions and the board runs on the owner's laptop; the verb and the route exist, the docstring names them and says what would let the run read them. Rejected: a laptop-side export committed into Hello Revenue's tree — a mechanism for that project to want, not one this plan can decide for it.
