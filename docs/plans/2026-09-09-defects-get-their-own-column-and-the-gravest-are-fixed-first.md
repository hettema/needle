## Intent

The owner scans the defects the way he scans every other stage of the work: as a column he can open and close, that says at its head how many are his, how many nobody has read yet, how many wait on a signal and how many are fixing themselves, and that lists them gravest first. Today the defects are a strip folded into Backlog that shows one number, holds its cards in the order they were filed, and cannot be opened without hiding every idea; and nothing on the board says how bad any defect is, so "fix everything" is the only order auto-fix knows, oldest first, while the queue outgrows the night.

Gravity is a judgment, and the board already has a seat that judges every defect once, independently, from its document and its source: the reading that verifies who fixes it (#59) and, since #74, whether its title can be placed. That reading answers one more question here, from the same paragraph every defect already carries, the intent it breaks and what the owner loses while it does: how the defect shows and what it costs him, on a scale in his words, and how often it bites. The column is ordered by that grade and auto-fix takes the gravest verified defect first instead of the oldest. A grade is a claim with a fingerprint like every reading: the words say what in the document selected it, a changed document is read again, and a reader of another make audits the first thirty.

What does not change: who ranks, who starts, and what the mark means. The grade never opens auto-fix wider than the mark and the reading do; a defect graded gravest and marked his stays his. Backlog becomes ideas only, which is what its birth ruling wanted ("Backlog should be for ideas in my mind, easier to scan that way", #10).

### 2. The reading that verifies a defect's mark also grades it, in the owner's words
The same reading (`board/brief.py::triage_brief`, `api/doors.py::triage`, the `needle triage` verb) answers a second question on a defect, from the document and the source it already holds and nothing else: how the defect shows and what it costs the owner, and how often it bites. The scale, four words that are his (ruling 2): **lies** — the board or a card shows something false, or work is reported done that is not, and a decision he takes is built on it; **loses** — work that should land does not, or a card cannot move, and a session's work is wasted; **costs** — a retry, a wait, a step done by hand, nothing false and nothing lost; **looks** — true and readable, only ugly or clumsy. And how often: **every time** (each session, read, close or beat it touches), **sometimes**, **once seen**. The grade lands as fields on `domain/triage.py::Triage` beside the result, with the reading's words for what in the document selected it; the door refuses a defect's result without a grade, as it refuses a mark's result without the title's verdict today. A document with no "intent it breaks" paragraph is graded from what it has, and the words say so. The grade shows on the card's face as one word with its why, in the sentence shape #75 settled, and the face ratchet reads it like every face sentence. Done means: on the fixture a defect's reading without a grade is refused and the card stays unread; a graded defect shows its word and why on its face; a changed document is read again and the grade follows the new text; `tests/ratchets/test_every_face_sentence_says_whose_move.py` passes with the new words on the test board.

## Rulings

1. **A column, not a page.** The owner, 2026-09-09: "I don't mind an extra column because I can collapse them and see the board in the way I want to see it." #10's ruling rejected a column for width and said a column is one definition away if he still wants it; this is that definition, and the laptop's width is paid by starting furled, as the archive columns do. Rejected: the page in the Triage page's shape this conversation proposed, because he reads the board as columns and a second surface is a second way to look.
2. **The scale is four words and a frequency, and the words are his.** lies, loses, costs, looks; every time, sometimes, once seen. The four are the doctrine's own discriminators: §6 (a partial result reported as whole is bad information) and §11 (or the board lies) make *lies* the gravest; §5's "silent or late" is why *lies* outranks *loses*, which is loud. Rejected: a number, because a number invites a precision no reading has and cannot be read against a written intent; free text, because it cannot be sorted. Proposed at the door; the owner amends the words on this card and nothing else needs to change.
3. **One reader.** The grade is a field on the reading that verifies the mark, never a second reading of the same defect: #74 set the precedent (the title's verdict rides on the mark's reading), and #87 holds one reader per kind. Rejected: a gravity reading of its own, which would double the readings on every defect for one more field.
4. **The order is the machine's and the owner does not rank it.** Grade, then frequency, then age; unread last. Rejected: unread first, so he sees the reading is behind — the head line's count says that, and an unread defect on top of a sorted column is a defect nothing has judged sitting above ones something has.
5. **Readings run with auto-fix off.** The dial is his ruling about what enters execution without him; a reading enters nothing. Rejected: a second switch for readings, because a switch for something that never acts is a switch he would never touch.
6. **Impact against the focus is #87's, not this card's.** This grade says how bad; #87's reading says whether a card moves what matters now. When #87 lands its lens orders this column like every other, and gravity stays the column's own order.

## Deliberately not

- Grading ideas. An idea breaks nothing; #87 reads what it moves.
- Sorting the readings queue by gravity. Nothing unread has a grade.
- Fixing Needle's own defects while other work is live. That is #43.
- A gravity a session sets by hand on the document. The document says what breaks; the grade is the second reading's, so the writer of a defect never grades its own.
