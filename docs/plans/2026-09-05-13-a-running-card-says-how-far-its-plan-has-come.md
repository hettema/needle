## Intent

The owner reads how far a running lane has come from the collapsed card, without opening it and without opening a terminal, and what he reads is the lane's own word. A plan's items are the task list. When an item's "done means" holds, the session writes its stance at the item in its own copy of the plan, in the commit that makes it true. The board reads that copy from the lane's worktree and the card shows the count: a strip with one segment per item, "2 of 4 met", and the last item marked. The marks fold with the code, so the archive keeps them and the review reads them against the work. The board never judges an item; it shows what the lane said and says so.

### 1. The grammar: an item's stance is written at the item, when it becomes true
`docs/plans/README.md` (the source every project's plan folder copies) says: every item ends with what "done" means; when that holds, the session ends the item with one line, `**Met:** <what shows it>`, in the commit that makes it true; an item that landed otherwise than written ends with `**Deviated:** <pointer>`. The why goes with it in two lines: the card shows the count of items met while the lane runs, and nothing but the session can know it; the stance is the close-out's, written early, so a close finds every promise already answered. Done means: the README carries the line and its why; the reader in `board/parse.py` reads a plan's items in both shapes the corpora use (`### N. Title` sections, as Needle's plans, and a top-level `N. **Title.**` list, as Hello Revenue's) with each item's title, its done-means sentence when it has one, and its stance — met, deviated, or none — with the text beside it; the inline habit (DONE, SHIPPED, ✅ on an item's line) reads as met, so the archive reads right without a rewrite; fixture plans in each shape, one per shape under `tests/fixtures/harbourmaster/docs/plans/`, read right; Hello Revenue's 500 archived plans read without error, with the count of items and stances per shape in the close-out. Hands out: `execution` runs the reader over Hello Revenue's archive and tables the counts; the lane verifies by opening three plans the table says are marked and one it says are not.

### 2. The board reads the lane's copy of the plan
While a lane has hands on a card, the loop reads the card's plan from the lane's worktree — the file at the lane's path, in the same read that takes the worktree's edits today (`api/loops.py`, where `runtime.edits(lane.path)` is read per live lane) — and the lane carries `Progress`, typed and mirrored: met, deviated, total, the last item marked with its title, and when it was read. Never the main checkout's copy: that is the plan as it stood at Start. Done means: on the two-lane fixture (`tests/api/test_doors.py`), a lane that writes `**Met:**` on item 2 in its worktree shows 1 of N on the next read with item 2's title, the main checkout's plan is unchanged, and a lane whose worktree is gone shows nothing; a plan with no items gives no `Progress`; the read is one file per live lane per beat, measured on the served board before and after and written in the close-out, with the prediction fixed now: under 2 ms per lane.

### 3. The collapsed card shows the count, in the lane's words
In Executing, the collapsed face carries the strip and the count between the essence and the state line, as the comp draws it: one segment per item, met segments in the live colour, a deviated segment outlined, the rest quiet; under it "2 of 4 met · last: <title>", truncated to one line. The state line, the door and the border are untouched. The open card gains one section, the plan's items in order with their stance and the text beside it — the evidence for a met item, the pointer for a deviated one, the done-means sentence for one not yet met — in every column, so a shipped card keeps the shape of its run. Done means: the signed canvas's artboards and manifest are committed under `docs/design/2026-09-05-how-far-a-running-card-has-come/` as the frozen record, the way the colour language's are, before the face is touched; an Executing card with hands on and a plan with items shows the strip and the count; with no stance it shows "0 of N met", never nothing; a plan with no items shows the signed card exactly; the open card lists every item with its stance; a vitest holds the face and the section; the served throwaway board shows the strip on a real Executing card, screenshot in the close-out; the colour ratchet passes because the strip names its meaning before it paints.

### 4. The brief tells the lane, once
The Start door's brief (`api/doors.py`, beside "Write back to the card through the `needle` command line") says: when an item's done-means holds, end it in your plan with `**Met:** <what shows it>` in the commit that makes it true, `**Deviated:** <pointer>` when it landed otherwise; the card shows the count as your word. ## Loop
We think a stance at the item, asked for once in the brief, will change whether the owner can tell how far a lane is from the face alone, because a session that is told writes as it works and the count is then true within a beat. Signal, fixed now: on the first five lanes started after the fold, the lane branch's commits that touched the plan — how many stances were written in a commit that also changed code, against how many landed in the fold's last commit or never. If four of five lanes wrote stances as they worked, keep. If lanes write them only at the end or not at all, the brief is not landing and the channel changes, not the grammar: the word (plan 10's channel) says once, when a lane's third commit lands with no stance in its plan, that the card shows 0 of N and why.

## Rulings
Recorded as the build makes them, each with the alternative rejected. The first four are from the conversation that wrote this plan.

1. **The card shows what the lane marked, worded as its word, never as the board's verdict** (owner ruling 2026-09-05: "the progress of the task list as the live session reports it"). Rejected: the board inferring progress from commits or the transcript — a commit does not name an item, a lane that commits once at the end would show nothing until the fold, and an inference is the board's verdict on work it cannot judge.
2. **The mark is the close-out stance, written early; one grammar, not two.** Rejected: a checkbox — no plan in either corpus uses one (0 of 14 live at Hello Revenue), and a box carries no evidence; the stance carries what the review reads.
3. **The lane's copy, never the trunk's.** Rejected: waiting for the fold to read the plan — the fold is the moment the count stops mattering.
4. **Met segments take the live colour, the lane's own; nothing on the strip is green.** Green is the board's word for a loop it closed itself, and a met item is a claim the review has not yet read.
