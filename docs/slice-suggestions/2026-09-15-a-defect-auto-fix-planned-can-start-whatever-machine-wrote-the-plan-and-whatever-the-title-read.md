## The intent it breaks

When you turn auto-fix on, a verified defect gets a plan and a session without you, and the card moves with the work. Today the first card the switch took got its plan and then became yours with its start closed: the plan was pushed from the rented machine, the board waited two minutes for it while it levels that clone every five, called the planning session ended "without a plan", and then read the plan in — but by its own rule a card the dial took once is the owner's from there, and the plan's title, written under the same words the cold reading had already refused on the suggestion, failed the reading again, so Start is closed on a card nobody will rewrite. What you lose: a slot of the four spent on a card that ends on your desk unstartable, with the reading's advice addressed to a writer who has gone, and a "fixing themselves" count that stalls while it looks busy.

## What done looks like

A planning session's turn is settled against the corpus as it will be read, not against a two-minute clock: the dial waits for the trunk loop to level the machine the session ran on (or levels it itself) before calling the session ended without a plan, and a plan that arrives after a wrongly ended lane re-opens that lane instead of making the card the owner's. And a planning session for a card whose title a cold reading refused is handed the reading's words and the owner's test at the top of its brief, so the plan it writes carries a title in his words; a plan whose title still fails is read again like any title, and the dial does not count the lane ended until the reading has run. On the test board: a planning session that pushes its plan from a second machine, levelled four minutes later, is not ended and its card starts; a card whose suggestion's title failed gets a plan whose brief quoted the failure, and its Start opens when the new title passes; a lane ended before its plan arrived is re-opened when the plan does.
