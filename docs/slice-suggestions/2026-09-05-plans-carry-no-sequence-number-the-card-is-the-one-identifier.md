# Plans carry no sequence number; the card is the one identifier

**Found by:** the owner, from Hello Revenue's Idea door on 2026-09-05 (conversation bbd1dd9c), after "plan 15" turned out to be omarchy #17 and "plan 08" Needle #20: "why do we have plan numbers and card numbers that aren't the same? Feels like a confusion root cause we should fix?"
**Kind:** idea
**Fix:** his

## Observation

Needle's and the machine's plans carry a sequence number in the filename (`2026-09-05-16-every-loop-…`, `2026-09-05-15-the-plan-shape-…`) and cite each other by it in prose ("plan 12", "08, 13"). The board numbers cards at birth, per project, and suggestions take numbers too. The two sequences agree until the first suggestion is carded and then drift for good: on the machine board two Spotify suggestions took #13 and #15, so plan 15 is card #17; on Needle's board plan 08 is #20, plan 12 is #36, plan 16 is #42. The machine's plan 15 says of itself "the number is the next after card 14", which is the confusion in one line. Neither repository's plans README states the filename-number rule; it is an unwritten convention with no reader.

## Why it matters

One thing with two identifiers, born in two places, is the manual-registry drift the doctrine refuses. Every "plan N" in prose is a lookup the reader has to do against a table nobody holds, and the owner clicks cards, not filenames. Hello Revenue never numbered plans — dated stems only — and has no such confusion; #411 there cites the other repositories' work by card number for that reason.

## What would fix it

New plans in Needle and the machine repository take dated stems with no sequence number, the two READMEs say so and why, and prose cites a card (`#20`) or a stem, never "plan 08". Existing filenames stay (a rename costs every citation; `cites_of` and the card's link read the stem) — the convention ends going forward. What this does not touch: card numbers, which the board owns.
